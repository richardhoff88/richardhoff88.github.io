AnchorLock: steering a video model with its own quick draft

TLDR: Run the model for just two denoising steps from the same noise, and you get a blurry draft that often avoids the physical mistakes the full sample makes. Nudge the early steps of the full sample toward that draft.

Richard Hoffmann November 9, 2026 Work in progress

Give a video model the same first frames and prompt, and it will sometimes hand back a clip in which things go wrong: a ball rolls out of a pipe, and then a second ball rolls back in from nowhere. The mistake is usually committed early, while the sample is still mostly noise. The model's own two-step draft, which is cheap and blurry, tends not to contain it.

AnchorLock uses that draft as an anchor. It is a small change to the sampler. It needs no training, no verifier, and no second model. This page walks through the idea, then lets you watch it work on one episode.

The idea

Start from one noise sample. First, run only two denoising steps and decode the result: this is the anchor. It is blurry, but it keeps the coarse story of the video. Then run the normal 30-step sampler from the same noise. At each early step, while the noise level σ is high, the model predicts a clean video x̂0. AnchorLock moves that prediction part of the way toward a coarse version of the anchor, an average over blocks of about 160 pixels, so only the large-scale layout is pulled. The pull is full strength down to σ = 0.8, fades to zero by σ = 0.7, and from then on the sampler runs unaided. The detail is the model's own.

The anchor only costs two extra denoising steps out of thirty, on the same noise.

Across 198 episodes

I ran the same setting on all 198 Physics-IQ episodes with LTX-2.3 (22B), continuing the video from the conditioning clip, and scored each with the benchmark's own metrics.

Physics-IQ score, mean over 198 episodes
  • Baseline (seed 0): 0.336. AnchorLock: 0.389.
  • Paired difference: +0.053 (95% CI +0.038 to +0.068); AnchorLock wins on 69% of episodes.
  • Against the 8-seed average of the baseline per episode (0.324): +0.065 (CI +0.049 to +0.082), 68% wins.

Caveats

  • Calmer videos. The median AnchorLock video has about 67% of the baseline's pixel motion, and the gain is larger where motion dropped more (correlation −0.38). Some of the improvement may come from the videos being quieter, not from them being more correct. Separating the two is the next thing to check.
  • Chosen on this benchmark. The strength and the fade window are the best of the settings I tried on Physics-IQ itself, so the numbers above are in-sample for that choice.
  • One model, one benchmark. Everything here is LTX-2.3 on Physics-IQ.
  • Cost. Two extra steps out of thirty is a back-of-the-envelope figure for the added compute, not a timing I have measured.
